What it costs

$24,000 a month becomes $7,375 with the salary counted. That is $199,500 a year, or 56 per cent of the bill.

The reference build is 5 machines and a spare on the shelf, in one cage. Every figure below is a public list price observed while writing, against a bill this size. Substitute your own and the shape does not change.

The people row Both columns carry it, because both columns have it. A cloud invoice bills for machines, not for the person who upgrades the managed cluster, rotates the credentials, chases the bill and carries the pager — about 60 hours a month, or $5,491. Owning adds 20 hours on top of that, not a whole extra person: the measured figure on a real two-site fleet larger than this one is fourteen engineer-hours a month, and this book books twenty because the people who measured it had done it before. That fourteen was measured and published by the company that publishes this book, so take it as corroboration rather than as proof; the twenty stands on an independent estimate of ten to twenty hours for a stack self-hosting its own database, cluster and cache, and this takes the top of it. Vendors selling managed clusters will tell you half an engineer to two — but their itemised effort is setup, which this book already prices once as the 80 person-days in the roadmap. A table that puts the salary on one side only is the reason repatriations get approved and then regretted, whichever side it leaves it off.

The row under it $2,310 a month never comes home and was missing from this table until it was checked: the content network, outbound mail and edge scrubbing that Move 04 tells you to keep renting, plus the residue five later Moves leave behind — archived object storage, a registry, a queue, an off-site backup copy. All of it is inside the $24,000 on the left, so a comparison that drops it from the right invents $2,310 a month of saving. It is the same error as leaving out the salary, in a smaller coat.

Five years, three ways

A month is the wrong window for this decision. Owning is capital on day one and cheap running afterwards; renting is no capital and dearer running; the cloud is no capital and dearest running. Compared a month at a time the capital either vanishes into an amortisation line or sits there looking like the whole story, and neither is what somebody signing the cheque is choosing between. So here is the life of one generation of machines, with the salary counted on all three sides and the capital on the line where it actually happens.

The residual After sixty months the owned machines are five years old and still working — hardware of this class is routinely run for seven or eight. What you hold is a fleet with years left in it, counted here at a conservative fifteen per cent of what it cost. It is also the whole of the difference between owning and renting at this size: strip the residual out and the two columns are the same number. You do not own hardware to save money against renting it. You own it when the fleet is big enough to carry the room, and to stop asking somebody else for permission.

Before you sign anything

Almost nobody should sign a facility contract before they have run this stack once. 3 refurbished machines on a managed switch under a desk cost about $3,430 once and $30 a month in electricity, and they run the whole of Stage 3 unchanged. It saves nothing, and that is not what it is for: it is the smallest bet that tells you whether the rest of this book is for you.

Every figure on this page is a dated list-price observation, not a quotation. Cloud prices are on-demand and undiscounted on purpose; a reader with a commitment discount should substitute their own effective rate, and a reader without one is genuinely paying this.

The rented column, with a warning The monthly rent of one dedicated machine is the single figure in this book that a re-check could not settle: the cheapest European provider and a survey of three others disagree by a factor of two or three on the same specification, which is wide enough to decide the question on its own. The number above is the last one observed and it is not a quotation. Get a quote before you let this column decide anything — Move 07 step 6 is where to do it.
