WEEK 63

Fixes the term, the escalator, the auto-renewal notice and the relocation clause, and writes the exit as a runbook with its notice date in the calendar.

Leaving from

AWSon-demand and Savings Plansleaving was a matter of stopping instances, with the commitment as the only thing that outlived the decision.
Google Cloudon-demand and Committed Use Discountsthe same, with the commitment attached to a project or an organisation rather than to a building.
Azurepay-as-you-go and reservationsagain, exit by deletion, which is the property that makes a facility term feel so unfamiliar.

Why this works

The cloud let you leave by deleting things. A facility agreement does not, and the shape of the exit is decided at signature rather than at departure. Four terms carry almost all of it: the initial term and whether it matches the depreciation schedule the bill of materials assumed, the annual escalator, the auto-renewal and the notice required to stop it, and the conditions under which the facility may move you within the building or out of it. The fifth thing is not a clause at all but a date in a calendar, because an agreement that rolls into a further term because nobody diarised the notice is the commonest way this saving quietly disappears.

The runbook

  1. Match the initial term to the depreciation schedule from Move 20. A five-year hardware life against a three-year facility term means a renegotiation halfway through the asset's life, which is a weak position; a term longer than the hardware life is a commitment to a building you may not want.
  2. Fix the escalator. An uncapped index-linked increase is an open cheque; a fixed percentage is predictable. Ask for a cap either way and put the resulting figure into the three-year model.
  3. Find the auto-renewal and the notice period, which is often ninety or one hundred and eighty days. Put the notice date in the shared calendar the day the agreement is signed, with two reminders before it.
  4. Read the relocation clause. Facilities reserve the right to move customers within a building and occasionally between buildings. Ask what notice you get, who pays, and whether your cross-connects are re-provisioned at their cost.
  5. Write the exit as a runbook rather than as a clause: space returned to a bare cabinet, cross-connects formally disconnected and their fees paid, the final invoice reconciled, who does the de-racking and where the equipment goes. Move 38 handles the drives.
  6. Publish the one-page summary to the team. The people who will be affected by the notice date are not the people who signed, and that is exactly how a notice date gets missed.
Rollback

An unsigned agreement is reversed by not signing it, and this Move deliberately happens before the term commences for that reason. The point of no return is the commencement date, after which the exit is governed by the notice period rather than by a decision. Keep the executed agreement, the one-page summary and the calendar entries in one place, so the commercial position can be restored quickly when a renewal notice arrives and nobody remembers what was agreed.

Operator’s notes

Swap

Where a longer term buys a materially better rate, take it only if the exit and relocation terms are good. A cheap five-year agreement in a building that can move you is not cheap.

Do it faster

Ask for the facility's standard agreement early, before pricing. Reading it takes a day and it tells you which battles are worth fighting.

Watch out

Some agreements incorporate a facility handbook or rules by reference, and that document can be changed unilaterally. Ask for the current version and ask what happens when it changes.

Leftovers

Disconnection fees for cross-connects are frequently forgotten in exit budgets. Move 26 priced them; carry the number into the exit runbook here.
