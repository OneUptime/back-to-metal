20 Moves that take a startup off AWS, Google Cloud or Azure and onto hardware you own.

Written for a company with two or three engineers and a cloud bill around $24,000 a month. About 79 days of work spread across 34 weeks, and most of that is waiting for hardware rather than working.

The whole plan, on one page

5 stages, run in order, and every dependency points at a lower number. 18 of the 20 Moves are invisible to a user; the whole programme costs 25 minutes of downtime between them, and every one of them can be undone.

How long it takes

The labour is about 79 person-days. With 2 engineers it lands in roughly 34 weeks, and the shortest it could possibly take, with as many people as you care to put on it, is 33. The gap between those two numbers is not labour. It is the circuit order, the hardware lead time and the thirty-day windows a Move has to sit through before the next one may start, and nobody is working during any of it.

One bar per Move, drawn from each Move’s own effort figure and the dependency graph rather than from a plan somebody typed. The outlined bars are the critical path. Adding people barely moves the end date: 1 engineer about 35 weeks, 2 engineers about 34 weeks, 3 engineers about 34 weeks.
